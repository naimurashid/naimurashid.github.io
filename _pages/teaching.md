## Teaching Philosophy

I believe effective statistical training combines rigorous theory with hands-on computational practice. My courses emphasize reproducible research workflows, version control, and open-source tools that prepare students for modern data science careers in academia, industry, and government.

Through collaborative projects and real-world applications to genomics and clinical trials, students develop both technical skills and the critical thinking needed to communicate statistical concepts to interdisciplinary teams.

## Current Courses

### BIOS 735: Introduction to Statistical Computing

**Department of Biostatistics, UNC Gillings School of Global Public Health**

- **Role**: Course Co-Developer (with Dr. [Mike Love](https://mikelove.github.io/)) and Instructor
- **Format**: Required PhD course, 4 credit hours
- **Enrollment**: 30 students annually
- **Years**: Spring 2019 - Present

**Course Description**: Comprehensive introduction to statistical computing using R, covering data manipulation, visualization, statistical inference, simulation, optimization, and reproducible research. Emphasizes modern data science workflows with R Markdown, version control (git/GitHub), and collaborative software development.

**Topics Covered**:
- R programming fundamentals and data structures
- tidyverse ecosystem (dplyr, ggplot2, tidyr)
- Version control with git and GitHub
- Reproducible research with R Markdown
- Statistical inference and simulation
- Optimization and numerical methods
- Debugging and profiling
- Package development and documentation

**Resources**:
- [Course Website](https://biodatascience.github.io/statcomp/)
- [Syllabus](https://biodatascience.github.io/statcomp/syllabus.html)
- [Course Materials on GitHub](https://github.com/biodatascience/statcomp)

**Learning Outcomes**: Students develop proficiency in R programming, reproducible workflows, and computational problem-solving essential for doctoral research.

## Previous Courses

### BIOS 663: Intermediate Linear Models

**Department of Biostatistics, UNC Gillings School of Global Public Health**

- **Role**: Course Developer and Instructor
- **Format**: Required PhD course, 4 credit hours
- **Enrollment**: 35 students annually
- **Years**: Spring 2016 - 2018

**Course Description**: Advanced treatment of linear models including multiple regression, ANOVA, ANCOVA, and mixed-effects models. Emphasis on model diagnostics, variable selection, and applications to biomedical research.

**Topics Covered**:
- Multiple linear regression and model building
- Categorical predictors and interactions
- Model diagnostics and transformations
- Variable selection methods
- Linear mixed-effects models
- Generalized least squares
- Longitudinal data analysis

**Computational Tools**: R programming with extensive use of simulation for understanding theoretical properties and finite-sample behavior.

### Mentoring Philosophy

I prioritize:
- **Independence**: Guiding students to formulate research questions and develop novel methodology
- **Collaboration**: Engaging with clinicians and biologists to ensure methods address real problems
- **Communication**: Training in manuscript writing, conference presentations, and grant preparation
- **Career development**: Tailored mentoring for academic, industry, and government career paths
- **Open science**: Teaching reproducible research practices and open-source software development
